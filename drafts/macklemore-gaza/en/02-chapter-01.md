# Chapter One: Who Is Macklemore?

*"O humankind, We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another."*
*— Surah Al-Hujurat 49:13*

This verse reminds us that differences among peoples, colors, and cultures are not grounds for superiority or hostility, but for mutual knowing. The story of this book is a story of that kind of knowing: a white man from Seattle, in America's far northwest, bound to Gaza by no shared language, faith, or lineage, who decided that its people's suffering was his personal cause.

### Early Life and Beginnings

His real name is Benjamin Haggerty. He was born in Seattle, Washington, in 1983, and is now forty-three years old. He began rapping in the mid-2000s and released his first album, *The Language of My World*, in 2005. In that same period he wrote a song called "White Privilege," reflecting on his position as a white artist in a genre born in Black communities — a question he returned to more than a decade later with a second part in 2016.

The road was not easy. Macklemore went through years of artistic stagnation and addiction before committing to sobriety, an experience he has spoken about publicly many times and which became part of his artistic identity. Many of his listeners believe it is this experience that made him more sensitive to the suffering of others, and less afraid of loss.

### An Independent Rise

With producer Ryan Lewis, Macklemore released *The Heist* on October 9, 2012, without the backing of a major label — a rarity in the American music industry. The album was a huge success; its singles "Thrift Shop" and "Can't Hold Us" both reached number one in the United States. At the 2014 Grammy Awards the duo won four awards, including Best New Artist and Best Rap Album.

He went on as a solo artist, releasing *Gemini* in 2017, which debuted at number two on the Billboard 200, and *BEN* in 2023. Music sources describe him as one of the most successful independent artists of the twenty-first century, with billions of streams.

### An Artist With Convictions

From the start, Macklemore has not hesitated to put his convictions into his songs: about consumerism, racism, and addiction. Among his best-known socially themed songs is one in which he took a position in support of same-sex marriage — a position many Muslims, including the author of these lines, disagree with. I mention this deliberately, because fairness requires that we know the man as he is rather than invent a flawless hero. We do not agree with him on everything, but we bear witness to a stand for truth in a just cause. That is the Quranic standard, as we will see in Chapter Nine.

### Friendship With Ed Sheeran

By Macklemore's own account, his friendship with British singer Ed Sheeran spans thirteen years; the two shared a stage at the iHeartRadio Music Festival in Las Vegas in 2014, performing one of Macklemore's songs with Sheeran on guitar singing the hook. That long friendship is what makes the story of his removal more painful and more complicated than a mere professional dispute between two performers.

### Why Does He Matter to Us?

An Arab reader might ask: what does an American rapper have to do with us? The answer is that Macklemore speaks to an audience our media never reaches — one that never hears our mosque sermons or our organizations' statements. A young man in Ohio or a young woman in Texas may never hear a word from an Arab analyst, but they will hear a song by an artist they have loved since childhood. When that artist says that what is happening in Gaza is an injustice, he opens a window in a wall that has long stayed shut.

In the chapters ahead, we follow how this man moved from silence to the podium, from the podium to song, from song to America's largest stadiums — and then to being removed from them.
