# Chapter Five: Two Nights at MetLife

<p class="verse">﴿مَن قَتَلَ نَفْسًا بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ أَوْ فَسَادٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَكَأَنَّمَا قَتَلَ النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا﴾</p>

*"Whoever kills a soul — unless for a soul or for corruption in the land — it is as if he had killed all of humanity."*
*— Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:32 (part)*

This verse establishes a great moral principle: a single soul is equal to all of humanity. Children's lives must never be reduced to numbers in a news bulletin. On two nights in September 2026, Macklemore tried to give those numbers their faces back in front of tens of thousands of people.

### The Loop Tour

The Loop Tour is Ed Sheeran's sixth concert tour, supporting his eighth studio album, *Play* (2025). It opened on January 16, 2026, in Auckland, New Zealand, and is scheduled to close in Mexico City on December 12, 2026, with fifty-nine shows across Oceania, North America, and Latin America. It has moved through America's largest stadiums with a rotating group of opening acts.

Sheeran says Macklemore asked to join the tour the previous year and that he agreed, out of respect for him as an artist, and that all of his support acts choose their own setlists.

### September 4, 2026

On Friday night, September 4, Macklemore took the stage at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, just outside New York City, as one of Sheeran's openers. He told the crowd that a big part of the reason he had joined the tour in the first place was to stand in stadiums like this one and say two words very dear to his heart: "Free Palestine."

He added that he wanted those words to be loud and clear, so that people from Gaza to the occupied West Bank would know they had not been forgotten. He said he believed every human being walking on this earth deserves exactly the same freedom.

His speech was not limited to Palestine. He also called for freedom for Cuba, Congo, Sudan, and Iran, and for everyone in America living in fear of Immigration and Customs Enforcement, which he described in harsh terms. He then performed "Hind's Hall" while footage of the destruction in Gaza played on the stadium screens.

### The Fear That Did Not Silence Him

One of the most thought-provoking things he said that night concerned fear. He mentioned feeling afraid on the plane the day before, and said that if he let that fear stop him from speaking his heart in order to keep his own comfort and the audience's, that would be the day he stopped going on stage.

That sentence sums up his philosophy: the stage, for him, is not only a place for entertainment but a platform for testimony. If he cannot speak the truth there, he has no need of it.

### September 5: Deliberate Repetition

The following night, Saturday, September 5, Macklemore returned to the same stage and repeated the same words. That was a message in itself: the first night had not been a passing outburst but a considered stand. He later estimated that across the two nights he stood before some ninety thousand people.

### The Humanitarian Context

These words were spoken against a harsh humanitarian reality. According to *Newsweek*, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification reported by mid-2025 that Gaza's entire population faced acute food insecurity, and famine was formally declared in Gaza Governorate in August 2025. In the West Bank, more than a thousand Palestinians had been killed since October 2023, including over two hundred children. A ceasefire brokered in October 2025 allowed more aid and some hostage releases, but each side accused the other of violations, and fighting continued intermittently.

In that light, what Macklemore said was not a call to violence but a reminder that the war had not ended for those living through it, even when it faded from the headlines.

### The Crowd's Response

Observers noted that the MetLife crowd appeared receptive to his message. Macklemore would later return to this point, asking: what if the shows had continued, with the crowd growing louder in support of Palestinian liberation and Sheeran implicitly supporting it — which artist might speak out next?

That question was never tested. The storm broke before Macklemore reached his next stop.
