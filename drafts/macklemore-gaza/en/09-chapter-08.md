# Chapter Eight: The Wave of Solidarity

<p class="verse">﴿وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ ۖ وَلَا تَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْإِثْمِ وَالْعُدْوَانِ﴾</p>

*"Cooperate in righteousness and piety, and do not cooperate in sin and aggression."*
*— Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:2 (part)*

Cooperation in righteousness is a human principle before it is a religious ruling. In the days after Macklemore's removal we saw a rare picture of it, as other artists chose to pay the same price in solidarity.

### Early Voices

Even before the official announcement, Foo Fighters guitarist Chris Shiflett defended Macklemore in an exchange on X with another guitarist who had raised the "etiquette" of an opening act, replying: "Maybe speaking out against genocide trumps etiquette" — and noting that the MetLife crowd seemed receptive.

After the announcement, Garbage singer Shirley Manson called the decision sheer madness, said punishing an artist for pleading for the protection of an occupied and persecuted people was wildly unjust, insisted his words were not antisemitic, and asked what had happened to free speech.

According to *Far Out* magazine, the Irish group Kneecap, the British duo Bob Vylan, Swedish activist Greta Thunberg, and others also voiced support for Macklemore.

### September 15: The Withdrawals

The defining moment came on Tuesday, September 15, 2026. Hours after Sheeran's statement attributing the decision to the promoter, his supporting artists announced, one after another, that they were leaving the tour. The Associated Press reported that Sheeran's tour of the Americas had been plunged into chaos.

**Aaron Rowe:** The Irish singer-songwriter was first. He said he did not take the decision lightly, that Ed was a friend who had changed his life and whom he could never thank enough — but that the Irish know all too well about genocide, forced famine, and violent occupation.

**Beoga:** The Irish traditional band, which backs Sheeran on six songs in his set, announced it was departing the rest of the U.S. tour, describing what happened, in its own words, as the silencing of Macklemore by Zionist lobbies. The band said it had been friends with Ed for more than ten years and would remain so, and that it believes in dialogue as a means of advancing the cause of the Palestinian people.

**Lukas Graham:** The well-known Danish band also withdrew, thanking Sheeran and calling it a difficult decision. In a post widely read as a reference to Robert Kraft's role, the band wrote that money does not give anyone the right to own the conversation, and that no one's bank balance should decide who gets to speak or which suffering may be acknowledged.

**Finneas:** The Grammy-winning American musician and producer wrote on Instagram, "Artists must not be silenced when they speak up for the oppressed," and announced he was withdrawing from his upcoming dates with Sheeran, adding that he stands with Palestine and its people. It was not his first public show of support for Palestinians.

With that, according to *Just Jared*, Sheeran had lost all of the opening acts for his North American leg.

### Irish Memory

It is striking that two of the departing acts are Irish. That is no accident. Irish memory still carries the scars of the Great Famine of the nineteenth century and centuries of conflict with British rule, and recent years have seen broad popular sympathy for Palestinians in Ireland. It is a lesson that peoples who have tasted injustice are quicker to recognize it in the faces of others. Macklemore himself is an American of Irish descent.

### What Does This Solidarity Mean?

When Macklemore alone was removed, the matter could be framed as an administrative decision about a "troublesome" performer. When the others walked away, the story became a larger question about free expression in American entertainment and the limits of what may be said on its biggest stages.

It is worth noting that most of those who withdrew took care to say they bore Sheeran no personal hostility and valued his friendship. This is a delicate moral achievement: to disagree with a friend's position without severing the friendship, and to make a costly decision without turning it into a personal feud.

In this way Macklemore's prediction came true. The loss was not his alone; it became a revealing moment. Instead of one voice being silenced, several were raised.
