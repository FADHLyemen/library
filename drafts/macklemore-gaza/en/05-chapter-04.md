# Chapter Four: Hind's Hall 2 and the Seattle Slip

<p class="verse">﴿ادْعُ إِلَىٰ سَبِيلِ رَبِّكَ بِالْحِكْمَةِ وَالْمَوْعِظَةِ الْحَسَنَةِ ۖ وَجَادِلْهُم بِالَّتِي هِيَ أَحْسَنُ﴾</p>

*"Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good counsel, and reason with them in the best manner."*
*— Surah An-Nahl 16:125 (part)*

This verse lays out a method for calling others to what is right: wisdom, good counsel, and reasoning in the best manner. A just cause is not carried by its justice alone; it needs a tongue that knows how to deliver it without handing opponents an excuse to escape its substance. This chapter tells of a moment when Macklemore succeeded artistically and stumbled verbally.

### The Song's Second Part

In September 2024, nearly a year into the war, Macklemore released "Hind's Hall 2," an expanded version of the original featuring Palestinian and American voices: Gaza-born rapper MC Abdul, Palestinian-American singer Anees, Palestinian-American author Amer Zahr, the Los Angeles Palestinian Kids Choir, and a youth gospel choir.

The diversity carried a clear message: this is not a cause for Arabs alone, or Muslims alone. A Palestinian child standing beside an American gospel choir in a single song is a miniature of what human solidarity can look like.

### The "Palestine Will Live Forever" Festival

That same week, Macklemore performed in his hometown of Seattle at a benefit festival called "Palestine Will Live Forever," which drew thousands and raised money for Palestinians. During the show, he led the crowd in a profane chant against America.

The clip spread quickly, and the chant became the headline instead of the festival. It was soon announced that Macklemore would no longer perform at the inaugural Neon City Festival in Las Vegas, scheduled for that November.

### Apology and Clarification

Macklemore posted a long statement saying that his thoughts and feelings are not always expressed perfectly or politely, that he sometimes slips up and gets caught in the moment, and that that night had been one of those times. He regretted that a historic event in his city, which brought thousands together for Palestine, had been overshadowed by two words.

Yet in the same statement he did not retreat from the substance of his position. He described watching events in Gaza as excruciating on a spiritual, emotional, and human level, expressed his disillusionment with his government's continued funding and support for the war, and affirmed that his support for a free Palestine was unwavering.

### Lessons From the Slip

I see in this episode an important lesson for anyone carrying a just cause, especially members of Arab and Muslim communities in the West:

1. Opponents look for the wrong word, not the right idea. An entire festival of solidarity was reduced to two words, and the conversation turned to "anti-Americanism" instead of Gaza's children.

2. Anger is understandable, but it is not a method. The verse that opens this chapter asks the one in the right not merely to restrain anger, but to choose the best way of conveying the truth.

3. Admitting a mistake is strength, not weakness. Macklemore acknowledged his slip without abandoning his position — a rare balance.

4. Words endure. Two full years later, this episode resurfaced in the campaign against him in September 2026, as we will see, and Robert Kraft's statement referred to what he called a "broader history" of rhetoric.

None of this means that a single phrase cancels years of principled stance. It means that the bearer of a cause is responsible for not handing opponents free gifts. At MetLife two years later, Macklemore seemed more attentive to this lesson: his speech there was built on the language of dignity and equality for every human being.
