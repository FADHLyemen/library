# Chapter Nine: Lessons for Arab and Muslim Americans

<p class="verse">﴿لَّا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ﴾</p>

*"God does not forbid you from being kind and just toward those who have not fought you over religion nor driven you from your homes."*
*— Surah Al-Mumtahanah 60:8*

This verse sets a clear foundation for how Muslims relate to non-Muslims who have not wronged them: kindness and justice. *Birr*, kindness, is a broad word encompassing goodness, loyalty, and gratitude. How, then, should we — Arabs and Muslims in America — relate to an artist like Macklemore?

### Lesson One: Loyalty Without Demanding Total Agreement

I noted in Chapter One that Macklemore holds positions we do not share. But the verse does not require complete agreement in values before we show others kindness and justice. Whoever stands with the oppressed deserves thanks for that stand, without that meaning we adopt all his views. Our communities need this wisdom, since some of us tend toward one of two extremes: rejecting anyone who differs from us in anything, or adopting everything said by anyone who supports us in something.

### Lesson Two: Precision of Language

The Seattle slip teaches that a careless word can undo great effort. In the diaspora we need language that clearly separates criticism of policies from criticism of peoples and faiths, and advocacy grounded in documented human rights and UN reports, not emotion alone.

### Lesson Three: Art Is Soft Power

A single song under three minutes long reached millions whom dozens of conferences never reached. Tom Morello praised it and placed it within a long tradition of American protest music. Our communities should encourage their gifted young people in music, film, and writing, and stop seeing art as a luxury; today it is among the most powerful tools for shaping public awareness.

### Lesson Four: Practical Support

Solidarity is not just liking posts. When Macklemore dedicated his song's proceeds to refugees, people streamed it heavily, and listening became giving. Artists who pay a price for their positions deserve an audience that attends their shows and buys their work, not to be left alone once the wave of admiration passes.

### Lesson Five: The Power of the Vote

In February 2024, more than one hundred thousand voters in Michigan's Democratic primary chose "uncommitted" to protest the administration's Gaza policy. I live in Michigan and know the weight this state carries. Macklemore's song, in which he declared he would not vote for Biden, met that mood. The lesson is that artistic voice and electoral voice reinforce one another, and an organized community is better able to make itself heard.

### Lesson Six: Make No Idols

It would be a mistake to turn Macklemore or anyone else into an infallible symbol. He is a human being who gets things right and wrong, and may one day change his views. Our loyalty is to a stand, not a surrender to a person. The priority is to build our own strength rather than hang our hopes on other people's stars.

### Lesson Seven: Keep the Victims at the Center

The finest part of Macklemore's statement, in my view, is his insistence that he is not the victim — that the real victims are the Palestinians. That is a lesson for us as we tell his story: it must not become celebrity drama. Hind Rajab and the children of Gaza must remain at the center of the picture.

### A Word From a Researcher

In my research I work with data from thousands of patients, and I know that behind every number is a person with a name and a story. The greatest danger in long wars is that lives become statistics. What Macklemore did in naming his song after a child is what we try to do in medicine when we remember that a cell is part of a patient, and a patient is part of a family.
