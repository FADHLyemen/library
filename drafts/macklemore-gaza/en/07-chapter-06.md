# Chapter Six: The Storm and the Removal

<p class="verse">﴿يَا بُنَيَّ أَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ وَأْمُرْ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَانْهَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ وَاصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا أَصَابَكَ﴾</p>

*"O my son, establish prayer, enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong, and be patient over what befalls you."*
*— Surah Luqman 31:17*

In Luqman's counsel to his son there is a striking pairing: enjoining good and forbidding wrong, followed immediately by patience over what befalls you. Whoever speaks the truth will be struck by something, and patience is not a separate virtue but the other face of an honest word. In this chapter we follow what befell Macklemore.

### The Petition

After the September 4 show, the Israeli American Council launched a petition demanding Macklemore's removal from the rest of the tour. It stated that this had been an Ed Sheeran concert — not a political rally, protest, or activist event — and that the issue was not Macklemore's right to political views, but where the line should be drawn when an opening act uses a global concert platform to advance a one-sided political agenda. It asked tour leadership to clarify whether his speech, visual content, and performance of "Hind's Hall" had been approved in advance, and to set clear boundaries on political messaging from the tour stage.

### Pink and Boy George

The circle widened when pop star Pink reshared a post from the group StopAntisemitism calling for Macklemore's removal and accusing him of alleging a "false" genocide and turning a mainstream event into a political nightmare against the Jewish state. That set off a second wave of headlines.

Pink later clarified that she did not object to the words "Free Palestine" themselves, nor to Palestinians advocating for their rights; that she had never asked for another artist to be silenced and was not asking now; and that she did not want anyone fired. But she said she wanted to be able to say, as a Jewish mother, that the moment had frightened her and people she loves, without being told her fear was mere propaganda.

British singer Boy George — who had himself drawn backlash for releasing an AI-assisted pro-Israel song — posted an AI-generated image mocking Macklemore and wrote that he had never been a fan.

### September 14: The Announcement

On Monday morning, September 14, 2026, Messina Touring Group, promoter of the U.S. leg, told *Rolling Stone* that venues on upcoming dates had notified it they would not allow a concert with Macklemore on the lineup; that this would have meant cancelling the tour and affecting hundreds of thousands of fans; and that after discussions with stakeholders, Macklemore would not perform on the remaining dates.

Macklemore had been slated to appear at eight of the ten shows remaining on the tour. The upcoming stops included Philadelphia on September 19, Gillette Stadium in Foxborough on September 25 and 26, and Tampa on November 7. The Tampa Sports Authority confirmed Macklemore would not perform at its show.

### Macklemore's Statement

Hours later, Macklemore posted a long statement on Instagram. He began by saying he wasn't sure how to start and would speak from the heart, and announced that Ed Sheeran and his team had decided to remove him from the tour.

Before going further, he wanted to say something he considered obvious: he was not a victim, Ed Sheeran was not a victim, Pink was not a victim. All of them had careers, money, safety, and audiences around the world, and whatever the consequences, they would go home to their families. The real victims, he said, were the Palestinian people — the men, women, and children who had been killed, starved, and displaced.

Macklemore then related that Sheeran had told him Robert Kraft, owner of the New England Patriots and Gillette Stadium, said Macklemore would not be allowed to perform in his stadium, and that Kraft had rallied other stadium owners to deliver an ultimatum: if Macklemore stays on the tour, you will not play our venues.

He said his conversations with Sheeran over the previous week had been difficult and had ended in fundamental disagreement. He described his friend as being in a very difficult position, his usual apolitical stance tested as never before. He said Sheeran told him the words "Free Palestine" and the image of the Palestinian flag had hurt many people he spoke with — and that he replied that if those words were more offensive to them than tens of thousands of Palestinian children being killed, there was a fundamental disconnect between where they stood and where he stood.

### Why Now?

Macklemore posed a pointed question: he had been saying what he said at MetLife on stages around the world for almost three years, so why was it a problem now? His own answer: because he was now sharing a stage with one of the biggest artists in the world, in some of America's biggest stadiums. At a certain level of exposure, "Free Palestine" becomes too much of a risk. Then he wrote a line that captured his view: "The stage becomes resistance."

### The Line That Went Viral

He closed with the words that appear in the image this book began with: "I didn't need 10 shows from Ed. I needed 2." Two chances, he wrote, to stand before ninety thousand people and say the words that had become too dangerous to say — Free Palestine. And if those words cost him the stage while turning the conversation back to Palestine, then it was "the most successful tour I've ever been on."

He also expressed hope that his friendship with Sheeran would survive, and said that questions of who wins the internet, who gets canceled, and who plays stadiums all become very small next to what is happening in Gaza and the West Bank.

Here the meaning of patience in Luqman's counsel comes into focus. Patience is not merely enduring loss; it is redefining it. For the one who speaks the truth, loss is not what he gives up, but what he would have given up had he stayed silent.
