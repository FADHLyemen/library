# Chapter Two: From Silence to the Podium

<p class="verse">﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُونُوا قَوَّامِينَ بِالْقِسْطِ شُهَدَاءَ لِلَّهِ وَلَوْ عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِكُمْ﴾</p>

*"O you who believe, stand firm for justice as witnesses for God, even if it be against yourselves."*
*— Surah An-Nisa 4:135 (part)*

This verse commands believers to uphold justice and bear true witness, even against themselves and those closest to them. Witness against oneself is the hardest kind, because the price is paid from one's own account. In this chapter we see Macklemore begin to pay it.

### October 2023: The First Statement

After war broke out in Gaza following the October 7, 2023 attack, Macklemore released a statement on the war. In those first weeks, many American celebrities avoided the subject or limited themselves to general phrases that would offend no one. The prevailing message in entertainment circles was that the issue was "complicated," and that silence was safest.

### November 4, 2023: Washington

On November 4, 2023, one of the largest demonstrations calling for a ceasefire in Gaza filled the streets of Washington, D.C. Macklemore was in the crowd, having come with his eight-year-old daughter, with no advance announcement. Then he found himself on stage, in front of the microphone.

He told the crowd he hadn't expected to speak, and that there were thousands of people there more qualified than he was to talk about Palestine. He recounted being told to be quiet, to "do his research," that the issue was too complex to say anything about. He said he had in fact gone back over the previous three weeks and studied, and admitted that he did not know everything — describing himself with a striking phrase: "I'm teachable." He concluded that he knew enough to say that what was happening was a genocide.

### Learning Before Judging

As a researcher, those words stayed with me. In science we do not require anyone to know everything before reaching a conclusion; we require that the evidence be sufficient to support it. There is a vast difference between "I don't know every detail" and "I know nothing." The argument that "it's complicated" is often used not to encourage inquiry but to postpone judgment indefinitely — until the tragedy is over and speaking up no longer matters.

What Macklemore did, by his own account, is what we teach graduate students: acknowledge the limits of your knowledge, study, and then have the courage to state what you have found, while remaining open to revision.

### The Human Rights Context

The word "genocide" did not remain the property of activists. In December 2024, Amnesty International published a report concluding that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza. In September 2025, the UN-appointed Independent International Commission of Inquiry reached the same conclusion, and in June 2026 the Commission issued a further report describing the deliberate targeting of children. Israel, for its part, has repeatedly denied committing genocide or war crimes, maintaining that its military campaign was self-defense after the October 7 attacks, and rejected the Commission's findings as distorted and false.

I set out both positions because the reader deserves to know where each side stands. But it is also worth noticing that Macklemore spoke in November 2023 — more than a year before these major reports. He got ahead of the institutions, and he paid for it.

### The Start of a Long Road

That moment in Washington began a road that would run for nearly three years. In his latest statement, Macklemore says that what he said at MetLife in 2026 he had been saying on stages around the world for almost three years. For him, this was never a passing position but a continuous commitment — as would become clear in his next song, which bore the name of a child.
