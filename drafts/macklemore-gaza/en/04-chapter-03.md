# Chapter Three: Hind's Hall — A Song Named for a Child

<p class="verse">﴿وَإِذَا الْمَوْءُودَةُ سُئِلَتْ ۝ بِأَيِّ ذَنبٍ قُتِلَتْ﴾</p>

*"And when the girl buried alive is asked: for what sin was she killed?"*
*— Surah At-Takwir 81:8–9*

This verse presents a scene from the Day of Judgment: the girl who was buried alive is asked — not her killer — for what sin she was killed. The question itself is a complete indictment, for there was no sin at all. This chapter begins with another little girl who had committed no sin either.

### Hind Rajab

In late January 2024, six-year-old Hind Rajab was in a car with several relatives in Gaza City when the car came under fire from Israeli forces, according to press reports. Those with her were killed, and Hind was left alone among them, speaking by phone for hours with dispatchers from the Palestine Red Crescent Society, begging for someone to come. Two paramedics were sent to her, but contact was lost and no one returned. About two weeks later her body was found, along with those of the two paramedics in their ambulance nearby.

The recording of her voice spread across the world, and her name became a symbol for the children of Gaza. As a father, I could not listen to it to the end.

### From Hamilton to Hind

In spring 2024, student encampments spread across American campuses, protesting the war and demanding that universities divest from companies connected to it. On April 30, 2024, protesters entered Columbia University's historic Hamilton Hall in New York and renamed it "Hind's Hall" in honor of Hind Rajab. That same night, the New York Police Department cleared the building.

### May 6, 2024: The Song

Days later, on May 6, 2024, Macklemore posted a song titled "Hind's Hall," which he wrote and produced himself. He announced that all streaming proceeds would go to the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees (UNRWA). The song reached streaming platforms on May 11. Its release coincided with the start of Israeli military operations in Rafah.

The song addresses several themes: support for student protesters and criticism of the crackdown they faced; an attack on then-President Joe Biden for backing the war, along with a declaration that Macklemore would not vote for him; criticism of Congress's move to ban TikTok; and a distinction between anti-Zionism and antisemitism, with praise for the Jewish participants in the protests. Out of respect for the author's rights I will not reproduce the lyrics here, but anyone who listens will recognize that the track is closer to a political statement than a commercial single.

### The Impact in Numbers

According to *The National*, an UNRWA representative said Macklemore made an upfront donation of $100,000 to UNRWA USA. The song reached number two on Billboard's Rap Digital Song Sales chart and number seven on the all-genre Digital Song Sales chart. Within three days of its release, his Instagram following grew by more than half a million — at the very moment other celebrities were reportedly losing followers over their silence.

Some young listeners even invented a simple form of solidarity: leaving the song playing on mute all night, turning hours of sleep into small donations for refugees.

The celebrated guitarist Tom Morello praised the track, calling it "the most Rage Against The Machine song since Rage Against The Machine" — a reference to the American band famous for protest music.

### The First Performance

Two days after its release, on May 8, 2024, Macklemore performed the song live for the first time, in New Zealand. He told the audience he stood in solidarity with the people of Palestine that day and every day for the rest of his life, believing that our liberation is bound up together. A Palestinian flag filled the screens during the performance, alongside footage of student protests and of Gaza.

### A Note on UNRWA

Fairness requires noting that UNRWA was controversial at the time. In early 2024, Israel accused twelve of its employees of involvement in the October 7 attack; the agency fired or suspended those accused, and several countries suspended funding. Later reporting, cited by *Billboard* from NPR, stated that an independent review found no evidence linking the agency to the attack, and many countries resumed funding. Macklemore chose UNRWA despite the controversy — a choice that shows he was not looking for the safest path.
